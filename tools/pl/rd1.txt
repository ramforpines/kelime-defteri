=== B1 | A new hobby
P: Three years ago, I had never even thought about rock climbing. I was working long hours in a bank and spending most of my free time on the sofa. Then a friend persuaded me to try a beginners' session at an indoor climbing centre. I was terrified at first — I'm not very good with heights — but by the end of the evening, I was hooked.
P: What I love most about climbing is that you can't think about anything else while you're doing it. All your attention is on the next hold and the next move. After a stressful day at work, it's the perfect way to clear my head. I've also made a lot of new friends, because climbers always work in pairs and you have to trust your partner completely.
P: Last summer I climbed outdoors for the first time, in the mountains of northern Spain. It was very different from the climbing wall: the rock was sharp, the weather kept changing, and there were no coloured marks to show the way. It was the hardest thing I've ever done, but also the most rewarding.
Q: How did the writer feel during the first climbing session?
* Scared at first, but then very enthusiastic.
- Bored, because it was too easy for him.
- Confident, because he was used to heights.
Q: According to the writer, why is climbing good after work?
* It makes him focus completely on something else.
- It gives him a chance to talk about his problems.
- It is the only exercise he has time for.
Q: Why has climbing helped the writer make friends?
* Climbers depend on each other.
- His colleagues at the bank also climb.

Q: What does the writer say about climbing in Spain?
* It was difficult but worth it.
- It was easier than climbing indoors.

=== B1 | The four-day week
P: A small software company in Manchester has been trying out a four-day working week for the past six months. Staff work from Monday to Thursday and receive the same salary as before. The company's director, Helen Ward, says the experiment has been a great success.
P: "We were worried that we wouldn't get as much done," she explains, "but in fact productivity has gone up. People are more focused because they know they have less time. We've also cut the number of meetings, and most of them now last fifteen minutes instead of an hour."
P: Not everyone is completely happy, however. Some employees say that their working days are now more intense and that they feel under pressure to finish everything by Thursday evening. A few customers have also complained that they can't contact the company on Fridays. To solve this, the company now has a small team that answers urgent calls on Fridays, and those staff take Monday off instead.
Q: What was the director's main concern before the experiment?
* That less work would be done.
- That staff would earn less money.
- That there would be too many meetings.
Q: Why does the director think productivity has improved?
* Staff concentrate harder in the time they have.
- The company has employed a lot of new people recently.
- Staff now work longer hours on Fridays.
Q: What problem do some employees mention?
* Their days feel more stressful than before.
- They now have to work on Fridays as well as other days.
- They no longer have enough meetings.
Q: How has the company dealt with customers' complaints?
* Some staff work on Fridays and rest on Mondays.
- Customers can now only call on Thursdays.
- The company has gone back to a five-day week.

=== B1 | The secret of a good night's sleep
P: Many of us have trouble sleeping from time to time, but for some people it's a serious problem that affects their health and their work. Sleep expert Dr Mira Patel has some simple advice. "The most important thing," she says, "is to go to bed and get up at the same time every day — even at weekends. Your body likes routine."
P: She also recommends avoiding phones and laptops for an hour before bed. "The light from screens tells your brain that it's still daytime," she explains. "Reading a paper book is much better." Coffee is another problem: its effects can last for up to eight hours, so a cup in the afternoon can stop you sleeping at night.
P: Finally, Dr Patel says that if you can't fall asleep after twenty minutes, you should get up and do something relaxing in another room. "Lying in bed worrying about not sleeping only makes things worse."
Q: Why does Dr Patel advise keeping the same sleeping times at weekends?
* The body works better with a regular pattern.
- People have more time to sleep at weekends.
- It is easier to relax when you are not working.
Q: What is the problem with screens before bed?
* They make the brain think it isn't night.
- They are more tiring for the eyes than books.
- They make people want to drink coffee.
Q: What should you do if you can't fall asleep?
* Leave the bedroom for a while.
- Stay in bed and try to relax.
- Have a hot drink and read your phone.

=== B1 | Living abroad

- The low temperatures.
- The heavy snow.
Q: Why was it hard for Marco to practise Norwegian?
* People spoke English to him.
- Nobody understood his accent.
- He was too busy with his job.
Q: How does Marco feel about Norway now?
* He is happy and wants to stay.
- He is planning to return to Italy.
- He still finds the summers too short.
